1. The vision

DOJ aims to help make the global trade of jewellery and other valuable decorative goods more accessible, transparent and efficient. The long-term concept is a connected digital environment where buyers, merchants and service providers can discover products, review available information, communicate, manage orders and use appropriate payment services.

The project is designed around a practical commerce challenge: valuable goods often move through fragmented channels, where product details, seller credentials, certificates, transaction terms, delivery and payment information may be difficult to compare or verify.

2. The proposed ecosystem

Merchant network

Business profiles, merchant onboarding, product catalogues and tools for managing enquiries and orders.

Product information

Structured jewellery data, supporting documents and clear labels distinguishing seller declarations from independent assessments.

Online marketplace

Product discovery, buyer enquiries, quotations, order status and customer-support workflows.

Payment connections

Potential integration with appropriate payment providers to support secure and transparent international transactions where permitted.

4. Development plan

The initial programme covers 24 months. The first public distribution or offer of DOJ and the public release of marketplace functions are separate workstreams, each subject to its own legal, technical and operational readiness checks.

PHASE 01 · NOV 2026 – APR 2027

Foundations

Market validation, product scope, legal assessment and technical design.

PHASE 02 · MAY – OCT 2027

Pilot build

Marketplace prototype, merchant tools, product records and controlled testing.

PHASE 03 · NOV 2027 – JUN 2028

Launch readiness

Security review, provider integration, legal preparation and target H1 2028 public release.

PHASE 04 · JUL – OCT 2028

Expansion

Measured merchant growth, service improvements and additional integrations.

The H1 2028 date is a target, not a guarantee. No public token offer or regulated service should begin before applicable legal requirements, required registrations or notifications, provider readiness, security checks and customer-protection processes are complete.

5. Trust, security and compliance

The platform is intended to distinguish seller-declared information, documents supplied by a merchant, issuer/reference checks and independent assessments. Blockchain may be used selectively for public token information or integrity references, but sensitive identity and commercial documents should be protected in suitable off-chain systems.

Payment processing, custody, foreign exchange, escrow and regulated financial activities should be performed only through an appropriate legal structure and authorised providers where required. Merchant onboarding, sanctions screening, fraud prevention, privacy, refunds, complaints and disputes must be designed for each launch market.

6. Risks and limitations

  • The marketplace and related ecosystem features are not yet represented as live.
  • There is no promise that DOJ will be listed on an exchange, gain value, be liquid or be accepted for purchases.
  • Digital assets may be volatile and users may lose some or all of the value they acquire.
  • Product documents may be incomplete, inaccurate, expired or fraudulent; independent assessment has a defined scope and is not an absolute guarantee.
  • Legal requirements vary by jurisdiction and may delay, limit or prevent a planned launch or service.
  • Technology, cybersecurity, payment-provider, logistics and operational risks may affect delivery.
